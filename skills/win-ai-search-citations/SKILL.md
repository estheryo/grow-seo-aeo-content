# Win AI Search Citations

Turn AI-search optimization into an evidence-led loop: observe what answer engines cite, compare the winning evidence with the target site, improve the smallest useful set of pages and authority signals, then measure the result.

## Operating principles

- Treat traditional search visibility as an input to AI retrieval, not a separate channel.
- Optimize for useful evidence, not keyword repetition or unsupported claims.
- Benchmark pages already cited for the target prompt before prescribing changes.
- Make each important claim understandable and supportable in isolation.
- Combine first-party evidence with independent corroboration.
- Separate observed facts, source-reported findings, hypotheses, and recommendations.
- Never present changing or third-party ranking claims as guaranteed causal factors.

Read [references/method.md](references/method.md) when conducting an audit or writing a page brief. Read [references/sources.md](references/sources.md) when tracing a rule to its source or adding new source material.

## Workflow

### 1. Define the recommendation surface

Record:

- products, services, markets, locations, and buyer segments;
- high-value questions across problem, category, comparison, alternative, pricing, implementation, proof, and objection stages;
- answer engines and countries to test;
- the conversion event that matters after discovery.

Create a prompt set that reflects real buyer language. Include short category queries and detailed personalized questions. Do not optimize only for a single broad “best” query.

### 2. Establish a citation baseline

For each priority prompt, record the answer, cited domains, cited URLs, recommendation position, query variant, engine, locale, and test date. Repeat only enough variants to identify stable patterns; do not imply deterministic rankings from one run.

Also collect conventional search visibility, branded search, relevant landing-page performance, referral traffic, and—when available—AI-feature impressions. Keep impressions, citations, clicks, leads, and revenue as separate metrics.

### 3. Benchmark the pages already winning

Inspect the pages the engines actually cite. Compare the target page against them on:

- entity coverage and category clarity;
- answer and evidence spans that can be lifted without losing meaning;
- intent match and buyer specificity;
- first-party facts, dates, authorship, methods, and sources;
- headings, definitions, lists, tables, comparisons, and concise summaries;
- freshness, crawlability, speed, internal links, and structured data;
- independent reviews, mentions, citations, and relevant backlinks.

Do not copy competitors. Use the comparison to identify missing evidence and unclear positioning.

### 4. Map prompts to canonical pages

Assign each prompt cluster to one primary page. Prefer strengthening an existing page when it already owns the intent. Create a new page only when the intent, audience, or evidence package is materially distinct.

Cover the buying journey with connected page types such as category guides, alternatives, comparisons, pricing, use cases, industries, implementation guides, case studies, documentation, and original research. Prevent cannibalization by keeping one canonical owner per intent.

### 5. Build citation-ready evidence blocks

For each important question, write a compact block containing:

1. a descriptive or question-based heading;
2. a direct answer in the first two or three sentences;
3. one verifiable fact, example, constraint, or comparison;
4. the date, scope, methodology, or primary source needed to evaluate it;
5. a link to deeper first-party or independent evidence.

Keep sections focused enough to stand alone during retrieval. Use clean semantic HTML and ordinary crawlable text. Add tables or schema only when they accurately represent visible content. Do not treat `llms.txt` as a substitute for crawlability, authority, or useful pages.

### 6. Strengthen entity trust

Make the site consistently explain what the organization is, what category it belongs to, who it serves, where it operates, and how its claims can be verified. Prioritize legitimate evidence: documentation, transparent pricing, product specifications, customer outcomes, named experts, original datasets, reputable reviews, relevant press, partnerships, and editorially earned links.

For local businesses, additionally align the Google Business Profile, name/address/phone data, service and location pages, locally descriptive reviews, directories, community mentions, and location-specific proof. Reject doorway pages that merely swap city names.

### 7. Prioritize and implement

Score opportunities by business value, citation gap, evidence availability, page authority, effort, and measurement confidence. Prefer small, reversible batches. Preserve factual accuracy, disclose conflicts of interest, and require human approval before production publishing or outreach.

### 8. Measure and iterate

Track at least:

- prompt-level citation and recommendation coverage;
- cited URLs and cited passage themes;
- conventional rankings and branded search;
- AI-feature impressions when available;
- referral visits, assisted conversions, qualified leads, and revenue;
- page freshness and evidence validity.

Compare against the pre-change baseline and an unchanged control where practical. Re-check after indexing and on a fixed cadence. If visibility rises without business outcomes, improve the offer, proof, and conversion path rather than declaring success.

## Deliverables

Return:

1. a concise baseline and limitations;
2. a prompt-to-page map;
3. a winner-versus-target citation-gap table;
4. prioritized page edits and new-page briefs;
5. an authority and independent-validation plan;
6. a measurement sheet with owners and review dates;
7. explicit labels for observed facts, source-reported claims, and hypotheses.

Do not guarantee inclusion, citations, rankings, traffic, or revenue.

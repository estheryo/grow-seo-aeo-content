# Create People-First Content

## Overview

Turn a content idea, brief, or draft into a useful and trustworthy resource for a defined audience. Treat helpfulness as a verifiable editorial standard, not a word-count target or an assumed ranking trick.

This skill governs one content asset. For site-wide opportunity research, keyword portfolios, or SEO/AEO growth loops, use a broader site-audit skill first, then apply this skill to each selected asset.

## Operating Principles

- Write primarily to help a real audience complete a real task.
- Add evidence, analysis, experience, or examples that are absent from the baseline sources.
- Cover the promised topic completely enough that the reader can take the next step.
- Make authorship, expertise, sources, production method, and purpose inspectable.
- Never present inferred ranking mechanisms as confirmed facts.
- Treat information gain as an editorial heuristic, not a confirmed patent or standalone ranking signal.
- Treat bookmarks, shares, citations, engagement, and conversions as reader-value indicators; do not claim any one is a ranking signal without primary evidence.

## Workflow

### 1. Define the Reader Contract

State:

- intended audience;
- job to be done or decision to make;
- promised outcome;
- site or product relationship;
- primary next action;
- why this publisher or author is qualified to help.

If the topic does not fit the site's primary focus or the author lacks access to adequate expertise, evidence, or review, stop and report the gap.

### 2. Establish the Baseline

Inspect the relevant existing site pages, primary sources, and representative search results when available. Record what readers can already learn and which page currently owns the topic.

If an existing page already satisfies the same intent, recommend updating or expanding it instead of creating a competing page.

### 3. Build the Evidence Inventory

Before drafting, gather the strongest available inputs:

| Evidence type | Examples | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Primary sources | Product docs, standards, official data | Prefer for factual claims |
| First-hand evidence | Tests, screenshots, workflows, observations | Explain method and limits |
| Original contribution | Data, framework, calculation, comparison, example | Make the contribution explicit |
| External sources | Research, expert analysis, reputable reporting | Cite the canonical source |
| Expertise | Author bio, reviewer, relevant experience | Match expertise to the topic |

Do not invent experience, tests, data, quotations, credentials, or results. Label estimates, opinions, and inferences.

### 4. Create an Information-Gain Ledger

For every planned section, record:

| Section | Baseline already available | New value added here | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|

Valid new value includes original data, direct testing, first-hand examples, clearer synthesis, decision criteria, limitations, counterexamples, or a defensible point of view. Rewriting or combining existing summaries alone is not sufficient. Merge, replace, or remove sections with no meaningful contribution.

### 5. Draft for Completion

- Lead with a direct answer or useful orientation.
- Give each asset one primary topic and intent.
- Use an outline that follows the reader's decision or task sequence.
- Explain important entities, dependencies, tradeoffs, exceptions, and next steps.
- Make titles and headings accurately describe the content; avoid shock, exaggeration, and unsupported superlatives.
- Use the amount of detail the task requires. Never target an arbitrary word count.
- Use deliberate examples, visuals, data, or demonstrations when they materially improve understanding.
- Cite sources near the claims they support.

### 6. Document Who, How, and Why

Record:

- **Who:** author and reviewer, relevant credentials or first-hand experience;
- **How:** research, testing, data collection, tools, and meaningful AI assistance;
- **Why:** the reader problem the content exists to solve.

Disclose production details when they affect trust. AI assistance does not replace human verification, expertise, or responsibility.

### 7. Run the Publication Gates

Assign each gate `pass`, `repair`, or `block` and cite concrete evidence.

1. **Purpose:** A defined audience and useful outcome are explicit.
2. **Topic ownership:** The asset does not cannibalize an existing page.
3. **Original value:** It adds more than a paraphrase or stitched summary.
4. **Completeness:** It substantially fulfills the title and reader task.
5. **Experience:** First-hand claims are supported by real methods or examples.
6. **Trust:** Important facts are accurate, sourced, and free of misleading certainty.
7. **Who / How / Why:** Authorship, production process, and reader-first purpose are clear where relevant.
8. **Title fidelity:** The title is descriptive and proportionate to the evidence.
9. **Production quality:** Editing, structure, media, and presentation show deliberate care.
10. **Site focus:** The topic fits the publisher's audience and expertise.
11. **Reader satisfaction:** The reader can act without needing another basic explanation.
12. **Search-first risk:** The asset is not driven mainly by automation, trend chasing, traffic outside expertise, fake freshness, or arbitrary length.

### 8. Apply Stop Rules

Block publication when any of these remains true:

- important factual claims lack evidence or qualified review;
- first-hand experience is claimed but cannot be demonstrated;
- the asset is primarily an automated or manual summary with no substantial added value;
- unsupported claims about algorithms, patents, or ranking signals are stated as fact;
- an existing page owns the same intent and no consolidation plan exists;
- dates were changed without a substantial update;
- AI-generated material has not received meaningful human verification.

Run no more than two focused repair cycles. If a blocking issue remains, report what evidence, expertise, or decision is required instead of polishing around it.

## Required Output

Return:

1. reader contract;
2. overlap or ownership decision;
3. evidence inventory;
4. information-gain ledger;
5. outline, draft, or revision requested by the user;
6. publication-gate scorecard with evidence;
7. verdict: `pass`, `repair`, or `block`;
8. exact repair list or blocking dependencies;
9. author, reviewer, production-method notes, and canonical sources.

When auditing an existing draft, quote or point to the exact section behind each finding. When evidence is unavailable, say so plainly rather than inferring a pass.

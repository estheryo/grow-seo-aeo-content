# Sources and Interpretation Notes

## Author's Original Article

- Loki Yan SEO, “什么是Google HCU？如何创建实用、可靠、以用户为中心的内容” — https://x.com/loki_yan_seo/status/2094576318788833400

The skill adopts the article's practical framing: translate Google's self-assessment questions into editorial gates; require meaningful information gain, first-hand contribution, topic focus, trustworthy sourcing, and protection against search-engine-first production habits.

The article is a native long-form X article, so the X URL above is the author's canonical original rather than a duplicate social share of another page.

## Primary Guidance Used for Verification

- Google Search Central, “Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content” — https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content

This primary source supports the official self-assessment questions, the distinction between people-first and search-engine-first content, and the Who / How / Why framework. It also explains that E-E-A-T is a conceptual evaluation framework rather than one specific ranking factor.

## Interpretation Boundaries

- The skill does not adopt speculation that a particular Google NLP service update explains how helpful-content systems work.
- The skill does not treat Chrome bookmarks or other user behaviors as confirmed ranking signals.
- The article associates US 11,675,855 B2 with “Scoring documents based on information gain.” The referenced patent number instead identifies an unrelated constrained-optimization patent, so that attribution is excluded.
- “Information gain” remains useful here as an editorial test: identify what a page contributes beyond existing sources. It is not presented as proof of a specific Google ranking mechanism.
